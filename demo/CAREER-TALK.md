# Careers Without a Map: Why Your "Unrelated" Experience Is Your Edge

Lightning talk · ~13 min · no slides · story-as-evidence (lesson-first) · closing
discussion prompt.

> Delivery notes: no slides — lean on the story. Optionally "walk" the zig-zag in
> the air with your hand as a lattice, not a ladder. Lead every beat with the
> lesson so it never sounds like a résumé. One vivid feeling + one transferable
> lesson per beat. Rehearse the transitions — that's where lightning talks drift.

---

## 1. Hook — flip the assumption (~1.5 min)

Open on *them*, not you:

> "Quick show of hands: how many of you have a chapter of your career you
> quietly leave off — a role, a detour, a stack you don't put on your CV because
> it feels 'unrelated'?"

Then the promise:

> "I'm going to make the case that those detours are the most valuable thing you
> own. And I'll use my own pretty zig-zag path as exhibit A."

## 2. The claim (~1 min) — state the thesis up front

> "We're taught a career is a ladder — same rungs, straight up. The most durable
> careers I know look more like a lattice: sideways moves, restarts, odd
> combinations. My argument is simple: over a long career, **range beats
> linearity**. Everything I say next is evidence for that one idea."

## 3. Evidence — the journey in four beats (~6 min)

Each beat = one feeling + one lesson *for the audience*.

**Beat 1 — The DBA who said yes to code.**
> "I started as an RDBMS DBA — the person who guards the database. Then I got a
> chance to write application code in a Java backend. It felt like trespassing;
> I was the 'database guy' suddenly in the app layer."
> **Lesson for you:** the first uncomfortable 'that's not my job' step is exactly
> where range starts. Say yes to the thing that isn't your title.

**Beat 2 — Living in two database worlds.**
> "For years I worked across relational *and* MongoDB — as a DBA and in app
> support. I didn't drop the old skill to learn the new one; I stacked them."
> **Lesson:** you rarely replace expertise — you layer it. Being fluent in two
> models is what lets you reason about tradeoffs nobody who knows only one can
> see.

**Beat 3 — India to the Netherlands.**
> "After nearly a decade working in India, I moved to the Netherlands. New
> country, new network, new context — a full reset of everything except what was
> in my head."
> **Lesson:** the scariest move is often sideways or geographic, and it builds
> the most transferable skill of all — adaptability. That compounds forever.

**Beat 4 — Reinventing the stack again.**
> "I went from Java cloud backend to where I am now: a senior backend engineer
> in Node, Next.js, on AWS. 'Senior' didn't mean 'done learning' — it meant
> being a confident beginner, again, on purpose."
> **Lesson:** seniority isn't the end of reinvention. It's the skill of getting
> productive in unfamiliar territory quickly — and being okay feeling junior
> while you do.

## 4. Synthesis — name their edge (~3 min)

Zoom out from your story to something they can use tomorrow:

> "Look at the pattern. The DBA instinct makes me a sharper backend engineer.
> Living in two database worlds makes me fluent in tradeoffs. The country move
> built adaptability. None of it was planned — and all of it compounds. That's
> the thing about range: from the inside it feels scattered; from a distance it's
> a rare combination only you have."

Concrete takeaway (give them a verb):

> "So here's what I'd ask you to do: find the one experience you've been
> discounting — the 'unrelated' one — and ask what rare combination it gives you
> that a straight-line résumé never could. Then stop hiding it. Lead with it."

Permission line:

> "And if you're staring down a move that makes you feel like a beginner again —
> that feeling isn't failure. It's the tax you pay to stay relevant for
> decades. Pay it gladly."

## 5. Close — hand the mic to the room (~2 min)

> "I'd love to turn this around. **What's one 'unrelated' experience on your own
> path — and what edge might it actually be giving you?**"

Invite two or three to share. In an informal `.local` setting, this is where the
real connection happens — let it breathe, then thank them.

---

### Timing cheat sheet
| Section | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | 1.5 min |
| Claim | 1 min |
| Four beats | 6 min (~1.5 each) |
| Synthesis | 3 min |
| Close + discussion | 2 min |
| **Total** | **~13.5 min** |

### If you're running long
- Compress Beat 2 and Beat 4 into one line of feeling + one lesson each.
- Keep the hook, the thesis, Beat 3 (the move), the takeaway verb, and the
  closing prompt — those carry the whole arc.
